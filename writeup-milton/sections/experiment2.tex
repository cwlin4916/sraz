\section*{Experiment 2 \quad How informative are partial symbolic states?}

\section{Formalizing informativeness}

Fix a rollout policy $q$, initially uniform over legal actions. Starting from
state $s$, this policy induces a distribution over terminal rewards,
$Z_s=R(\Pi)$ with $\Pi\sim q(\cdot\mid s)$. There are several fundamentally
different summaries of $Z_s$.

\paragraph{Mean completion value.} $\boxed{\;\mu(s)=\EE[Z_s].\;}$ How good is a
\emph{typical} random completion? This does not say whether one excellent
completion is reachable.

\paragraph{Optimal reachable value.}
$\boxed{\;V^*(s)=\sup\operatorname{supp}(Z_s)=\max_{\pi\in\c E(s)}R(\pi).\;}$
How good is the \emph{best} completion?

\paragraph{Probability of discovering a good completion.} For threshold $\tau$,
$\boxed{\;p_\tau(s)=\Pr(Z_s\ge\tau).\;}$ Under independent random completions the
expected number of attempts to hit the threshold is approximately $1/p_\tau(s)$,
often more operationally meaningful than the mean.

\paragraph{Maximum of $K$ rollouts.} Given returns $Z_{s,1},\ldots,Z_{s,K}$,
$\boxed{\;\widehat M_K(s)=\max_{1\le j\le K}Z_{s,j}.\;}$ This is \emph{not} an
unbiased estimator of $V^*(s)$; it estimates a budget-dependent extreme value,
and its quality depends strongly on the probability of sampling a near-optimal
completion.

\paragraph{Upper-tail mean.} Let $Z_{s,(1)}\le\cdots\le Z_{s,(K)}$ be the ordered
returns and $m=\lceil\varepsilon K\rceil$. Define
\[
  \boxed{\; \widehat U_{\varepsilon,K}(s)
    = \frac1m\sum_{j=K-m+1}^K Z_{s,(j)}. \;}
\]
This empirical upper-tail conditional expectation---a reward-oriented analogue of
upper CVaR---interpolates between the mean ($\varepsilon=1$) and the maximum
($m=1$). Risk-seeking symbolic regression~\cite{petersen2021deep} uses this idea
because the objective is to generate a few exceptional expressions rather than to
improve the mean sampled expression. A recent symbolic-regression MCTS
preprint~\cite{huang2025mctssr} similarly frames the problem as extreme rather
than average reward allocation, though its results should be treated as preprint
evidence.

\section{Hypotheses}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6em, style=nextline]
  \item[H2.1 (positive-control informativeness).] In the additive grammar, the
  random-completion mean should be positively associated with $V^*$, because
  adding useful terms does not introduce division or nonlinear fitting failures.
  \item[H2.2 (deceptive-state failure).] In the full grammar on \texttt{quad\_B},
  $\operatorname{Corr}(\mu(s),V^*(s))$ will be much lower: states containing rare
  exact completions can have poor mean rollout value because most completions hit
  the $-1$ floor.
  \item[H2.3 (upper-tail advantage).] At matched rollout budget $K$, an
  upper-tail estimator will produce lower action-selection regret than the mean,
  $\EE[r_{\mathrm{sibling}}(U_{\varepsilon,K})]<\EE[r_{\mathrm{sibling}}(\mu_K)]$.
  \item[H2.4 (offline-to-online validity).] An estimator is useful only if lower
  offline decision regret predicts lower MCTS simple regret under a fixed
  computation budget.
\end{description}

\section{Stage 1: exact state census}

Use the same two environments E1 and E2. For every state $s$, store its
derivation depth, current token length, number of nonterminals, number of legal
actions, remaining token capacity, $V^*(s)$, $Q^*(s,a)$ for every legal action,
the number of reachable terminal expressions, and the fraction of reachable
terminals at the $-1$ floor. For E1, analyze every state. For E2, compute $V^*$
for every state by dynamic programming, but it is acceptable to perform repeated
rollout diagnostics on a fixed stratified sample---for example $250$ states per
depth stratum, up to about $2{,}000$ states. Do not sample states solely by
random trajectories: that distribution overweights states that are easy to reach
under the random policy and can hide difficult but important regions of the tree.

\section{Stage 2: offline estimator comparison}

For each selected state, use $K\in\{4,16,64\}$ rollouts and repeat $50$ times with
independent seeded streams. Compute the sample mean $\widehat\mu_K$, the top-$25\%$
mean $\widehat U_{0.25,K}$, the rollout maximum $\widehat M_K$, and the empirical
success probability
\[
  \widehat p_{0.999}(s)=\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^K\mathbf 1\{Z_{s,j}\ge 0.999\}.
\]

\subsection{Global ranking measures}
Report $\operatorname{Spearman}(\widehat v(s),V^*(s))$ and top-decile recall,
\[
  \frac{\bigl|\operatorname{Top}_{10\%}(\widehat v)\cap\operatorname{Top}_{10\%}(V^*)\bigr|}
       {\bigl|\operatorname{Top}_{10\%}(V^*)\bigr|}.
\]

\subsection{Sibling decision regret}
Global correlation is not the primary metric: MCTS must choose between legal
children of the same state. For estimator $\widehat v$, define
$\widehat a(s)=\arg\max_{a\in A(s)}\widehat v(T(s,a))$ and
\[
  \boxed{\; r_{\mathrm{sibling}}(s;\widehat v)=V^*(s)-Q^*\bigl(s,\widehat a(s)\bigr). \;}
\]
Average sibling regret directly measures whether the estimator would make the
correct local search decision.

\subsection{Stratified reporting}
Report all metrics by depth, remaining token capacity, branching factor,
$V^*(s)$, failure-floor probability and near-optimal completion probability. A
single aggregate correlation can otherwise hide the fact that an estimator is
useful near the root but useless in deeper states, or vice versa.

\section{Why the current max-of-random proxy is insufficient}

The current proxy script estimates ``best achievable'' by taking the maximum of
$80$ random completions and correlates it with the mean of $24$ other
completions. The two samples are separate, so it is not literally reusing the
same returns; nevertheless both quantities are governed by the same rollout
distribution. If an optimal completion is extremely rare, neither sample may
encounter it, and then $\max_{j\le 80}Z_{s,j}\ll V^*(s)$. The script can
therefore conclude that the mean accurately predicts ``best achievable'' when
both estimators are merely blind to the same rare optimum. The exact-enumeration
oracle is the appropriate ground truth.

\section{Stage 3: insert the estimators into MCTS}

Hold fixed the policy prior (uniform), edge backup (mean), exploration constant,
number of simulations, root noise (off), action-selection temperature (zero) and
$K=16$ completions per newly expanded leaf. Compare leaf estimators:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item mean of $16$ rollouts;
  \item top four of $16$ rollouts;
  \item maximum of $16$ rollouts;
  \item exact $V^*$ oracle, as an unattainable ceiling.
\end{enumerate}
Use $N_{\mathrm{sim}}\in\{16,64\}$ and $50$ evaluation seeds per cell. Because
every empirical method uses exactly $16$ completions at each expanded leaf, their
dominant evaluation cost is matched.

\paragraph{Primary outcomes.} Report
$r_B=V^*(s_0)-\max_{\pi\in\c E_B}R(\pi)$, exact-recovery rate, terminal
evaluations to first optimum, and distinct expressions explored.

\paragraph{Success criterion.} An upper-tail estimator succeeds only if (i) it has
lower offline sibling regret, (ii) it lowers online search simple regret,
(iii) its benefit is not explained merely by doing more work, and (iv) it remains
reasonably stable across seeds. Filtering more aggressively is not itself evidence
of informativeness; a maximum estimator can be highly variable and optimistically
biased.

\section{Stage 4: backup rules as a separate experiment}

The current code has two distinct aggregation points: \texttt{rollout\_mode}, how
terminal completions at a new leaf are aggregated, and \texttt{backup\_rule}, how
repeated values traversing an edge are aggregated. These must not be changed
simultaneously. After choosing one leaf estimator, compare mean, max, top-$k$ and
softmax backups. For values $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, the softmax backup can be written
\[
  B_\tau(z_1,\ldots,z_n)
    = z_{\max}+\tau\log\!\left[\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        \exp\!\left(\frac{z_i-z_{\max}}{\tau}\right)\right],
\]
which approaches the maximum as $\tau\to 0$ and the average at larger $\tau$.
Backup operators materially affect finite-budget
MCTS~\cite{khandelwal2016backup,dam2024power}. Because symbolic regression aims to
return one good expression rather than to maximize cumulative reward, final
performance should be reported in terms of simple regret or the probability of
identifying an $\varepsilon$-optimal expression~\cite{zhao2023simple}.
